# Customized Dashboards — Design

## Intent

The dashboard is the first thing people see of the product, and households differ a lot: one house and five cards; ten houses; or no interest in houses and cars at all and a focus on family medical needs. Today's dashboard is a fixed, hand-built layout. This feature lets an admin shape the dashboard to the household, with an editing experience that feels Apple-like: simple and fluid on the surface, powerful underneath. It should wow people with ease of use and customizability.

Enabled by a new feature toggle, **Customized Dashboards**, in Settings > Features. When off, today's dashboard is untouched.

## Goals

- A grid of **cards** the admin can add, remove, move (drag and drop), and resize (drag an edge, vertically and horizontally).
- **Edit mode**: admins enter it, rearrange and configure, then leave it. Outside edit mode the dashboard simply shows as configured. Non-admins never see edit controls.
- **Per-card configuration** via a gear in edit mode, for cards that have config (filters, display options), or by double-clicking (double-tapping) the card body, which does the same as the gear and nothing for a card without config (`lib/dashboard/open-settings.ts`).
- **Multiple instances** of the same card, each with its own size and config (e.g. ten Property cards, each filtered to one property, or one card showing all).
- **Distinct card types for genuinely different views** of the same data (e.g. Spending as headline trend vs category breakdown). A new card type exists only when it is a different view, not a re-skin.
- **Size is a lens**: widening or heightening a card reveals richer, useful content (more fields, thumbnails, lists, a chart). The admin should feel "this is worth keeping wider". Charts appear only when they are useful: enough data and enough room; otherwise show a number or list.
- **Site-wide layout**: one layout for the whole household, not per person. Only admins can edit.
- **Mobile friendly**, including editing on phones and tablets (the owner tests on Android phone and tablet).
- **Extensible**: more cards will come. Adding a card must be one self-contained module registered in one place.
- Looks like this site (its themes and card styling). Dockhand's dashboard is a functionality reference more than a visual one; draw on both while keeping the site's flavor.

## Non-goals

- Per-user dashboards or per-user layouts.
- Draft/publish or versioned layouts. Changes apply to everyone as soon as they are saved.
- Cards beyond the first-round catalog below (more later).
- Replacing or changing the existing dashboard when the feature is off.
- Cross-page widget embedding (cards live on the dashboard only).

## Anti-goals (a failure even if it technically works)

- A broken or confusing dashboard. This was called out as a big problem.
- Cards that overlap, disappear, or become unreachable.
- Edit mode that is hard to understand or leave.
- Empty cards with no explanation.
- Content that looks cramped at a card's minimum size, or decorative charts on thin data.
- Dragging that fights page scrolling on touch devices.

## First-round card catalog

Properties, Vehicles, People (only when health is enabled), Equipment, Rooms (only when rooms is enabled), Intake, Reminders, Expiring Warranties, Spending (at least two distinct views: headline trend; category breakdown), Upcoming Maintenance (due within 30 days), Records, Active Warranties.

Added after the first round (owner's decision, 2026-10-27): **Text** (free text with live `{tokens}`; see "Text card and tokens" below). The default layout's first row is a Text card (the greeting), which replaced the page's own header.

Added after that (owner's request, 2026-10-27): **Clock** (the device's time and today's date in the household's regional formats, see "Clock card" below). It takes the top-right two cells of the default layout.

Per-card size tiers (below are the designer's guesses; refine during planning):
- Properties: small = count; wider = thumbnails and status; large = per-property list.
- Asset cards (Properties, Vehicles, Equipment, People; not Rooms), as built (owner's decision, 2026-10-27): minimum size 1x1 (maximum 12x8 unchanged). The look follows the card's raw grid cells in the layout shown (w columns x h rows; no phone-width scaling, so a full-width phone card is 2 wide), chosen by `assetLook` in `lib/dashboard/avatar-layout.ts`:
  - 1x1: a single avatar (the item's round photo, or its initials).
  - 1 row, 2+ columns: a row of avatars; they become pills (avatar + name) when each slot has at least twice the room a circle needs (container width / slots in use >= 2 x (circle + gap)).
  - 1 column, 2+ rows: a column of avatars.
  - 2 columns, 2+ rows: a column of pills.
  - 3+ columns and 2+ rows: the sized tiers as before (photo mosaic from 3x2, per-item list from 6x4).
  No title, counts or footer in the avatar looks. Every avatar and pill links to its item; what does not fit (or is beyond the card's "Show up to N") becomes a final "+N" chip linking to the list. Circles are 40px (44px on touch) with 8px gaps, sized to the measured card.
- Spending: small = year-to-date number with trend; wider = category breakdown; large = month-by-month bars.
- Planned Spend (Money group, in the default layout; min 1x1, max 12x6, default 4x3, phone 2x3): what is coming and when. The figure is the Costs page's "Planned, next 12 months" (reminders with an estimated cost, `forecastSchedules`; same scope, Health gating and "today"), for a 6 or 12 month horizon (setting "Look ahead", default 12). Looks by size (large-grid units; phone width counts double): 1 column wide = the figure with the next item (a column 2+ rows tall adds the next date and cost); 2+ wide, 1 row = the figure and, with 230px or more, a forward strip of the months (400px adds the next item), else the next item on one line; 2 rows = the figure and monthly bars with this month marked, as the room allows the biggest month and the next three months, the headline beside the bars in a wide, short card; 3x3 = bars stacked by asset type with a legend and the mileage-reminder note; 3x4 = the biggest items (name, next date, total, link to the reminder) under the chart; 6x4 = chart and list side by side. A chart needs two months of planned spend (`plannedChartable`) and its chrome follows the measured plot (y-axis only in a tall, wide plot; month names, then initials, then none); with fewer, the figure and the items are shown instead. Nothing planned: "Add an estimated cost to a reminder" with a link to /maintenance (mileage-only reminders are mentioned).
- Equipment Life (add-on card, Assets group, not in the default layout; min 1x1, max 12x8, default 4x3, phone 2x3): how worn out the equipment is, from `lib/equipment-life.ts` (age counts from the install date, else the purchase date, against Expected life in years) shaped by `lib/dashboard/equipment-life-card.ts`. An item is *nearing the end* from 80% of its life and *past* it from 100%. Equipment without an expected life or a start date is not drawn; it is counted, and the card says how to add the data. Follows the property filter. Setting: Show (all equipment, or only what is nearing the end). Looks by size: 1x1 = the count past their life (else nearing, else the most worn item's share); 2+ wide, 1 row = that figure plus a status bar (past / nearing / fine) and counts once there is 190px; 2x2 = one bullet bar per item, most worn first (name, time left or over, bar with the end of life as its right edge, or a marker when something runs past it), as many rows as fit with "+N more"; 3x3 = adds years of life used and, when tall enough, the status bar and a count of equipment missing data; 6x3 and wider = the bars beside the cost of replacing what is past or nearing the end (the sum of Replacement cost) and the status counts. Colour is never the only signal: every row says how long is left or over. Rows link to the equipment page; the header to Equipment.
- Service Activity (Maintenance & warranties group; the card's own title is "Services"; in the default layout; min 1x1, max 12x6, default 6x3, phone 2x3): a calendar of the days service was recorded, a square a day in week columns (Sunday to Saturday), darker the more records that day (quartile levels over the days with activity, so equal activity is one shade). Counts service records by their date (UTC day), follows the property filter and Health gating. Settings: Asset (everything, or one property, vehicle or piece of equipment: a grouped select, stored as `TYPE:id`, config field kind `asset`; a deleted asset is flagged like a deleted item and the card says so) and Period (a typed month count, 1 to 36). Looks by size: 1x1 = the record count with the period; 2+ wide, 1 row = the calendar filling the whole card (the seven weekdays fill the height, as many recent weeks as the width holds, newest at the right) with the title always shown at the top left over the calendar (about 30% of the card's height, at most 64px): point at it and it jumps to the opposite corner so the days under it can be read, and back when pointed at again or when the pointer leaves the card (a tapped or hovered day shows its readout in the title's place); one column wide, 2+ rows = the same, turned (weekdays across, weeks down, newest at the bottom); 2x2 = the calendar with a one-line summary; 4x3 = month and weekday labels, a legend and a readout for the day you point at or tap (default line: the busiest month); 6x3 and wider = adds records, busiest month, last record and longest quiet stretch. Cells are never smaller than 5px (8px when labelled): the oldest weeks drop off first. An sr-only table lists the active days; an empty range says whether there are no records at all or none in the period.
- Health Visits & Expenses (add-on card, Health group, hidden with Health off; same sizes, looks and rules as Activity): the Activity calendar for people's service records (their visits and expenses), for all people or one (the standard Person item setting), 6 or 12 months. Counts by date with cost in the readout; leads to the person's Visits tab, or to People. Activity itself no longer counts people: its Asset setting offers properties, vehicles and equipment only, and its "Everything" leaves out people's records (a stale person pick reads as a deleted asset).
- Health Observations (add-on card, Health group, hidden with Health off; same sizes, looks and rules as Activity): the same calendar for health observations (`Observation.date`), for all people or one (the standard Person item setting) and the last 6 or 12 months. Follows the property filter's hidden people. Days are shaded the way the Observations tab shades them (`lib/day-ratings.ts`): by the worst entry that day, mild (1) to severe (5), an entry with no severity counting as mild, never by how many there were (six shades, the tab's `--cost-bar` mix; legend Mild to Severe; the hover readout says e.g. "Moderate day (3 / 5)"). The largest look swaps Busiest month for Severe days (rated 4 or 5). No cost; the card and its numbers lead to the person's Observations tab, or to People for everyone. Shares the Activity view (`data.kind` picks the words).
- Reminders: small = next item; larger = dated list grouped by urgency.
- Dated-item lists (Records, Active Warranties, Due Maintenance, Reminders, Expiring Warranties), as built (owner's decision, 2026-10-27): no bare count at small sizes; the total is a small badge (linking to the full list). The look is chosen by `datedLook` in `lib/dashboard/dated-list-layout.ts`, by height first, then width, in large-grid columns (a phone card's width is scaled by `PHONE_WIDTH_SCALE` like any sized tier, so a full-width phone card reads as 4 wide):
  - 1 row, any width: a hero carousel. One item at a time (title, asset, date or days left, urgency marker, cost when there is room), rotating every 6s; paused on hover, keyboard focus within, while pressed, while the tab is hidden, in edit mode and with reduced motion; prev/next arrows at the edges (small overlay buttons under 200px wide), page dots along the bottom (at most 7, a window around the current one), swipe (40px, vertical scrolling untouched) and Left/Right keys. The slide links to its item. Slides are the card's own items in its list order (Reminders/Expiring Warranties up to their "List up to" setting; the others up to 8). One item: no arrows, dots or rotation.
  - 2 rows, any width: the headline item (next due, latest record, soonest to expire) with its urgency, and up to two compact rows under it, as many as the measured room fits.
  - 3+ rows: a list: slim (name, short date under it) at 2 columns, condensed (one line: name and a short date chip; the rest in the tooltip) at 3, full rows (name, asset, cost, date) at 4-5.
  - 6+ columns and 3+ rows: the rich tiers, unchanged (the list; Reminders by urgency and Expiring Warranties by month from 6x4).
  Minimums: 2x1 for all five (Reminders and Expiring Warranties were 2x2), so each has a one-row phone preset. Default sizes are unchanged. Order: Records newest first, Active Warranties and Expiring Warranties soonest to expire first, Due Maintenance and Reminders most urgent first. A warranty within 30 days of its end is marked "soon" (amber).

## Text card and tokens (owner's decision, 2026-10-27; as built)

- **Card:** type `text`, label "Text", icon `Type`, ungated, picker group "Text". One look at every size (one tier, `text`); min 1x1, max 12x4, added at 4x1 (2x1 on phones). Config (`textConfigSchema` in `lib/dashboard/text-card.ts`; missing keys get their defaults, unknown keys are dropped): `text` (plain, 1 to 500 characters, not blank; default "Hello, {user}"), `style` (`h1`..`h4`, `body`, `small`, `muted`; default `body`), `align` (`left`, `center`, `right`; default `left`), `border` (default on). The server validates all four on save (`invalidCardConfig`, `normalizeCardConfigs`).
- **Rendering:** React text nodes and `next/link` links only (no HTML or markdown; the user cannot write a link, see "Links" below); line breaks kept (`whitespace-pre-wrap`), long words wrap. Headings are real `h1`..`h4` elements in the site's heading font, at the page-heading sizes (H1 is the old greeting's `font-heading text-xl font-semibold tracking-tight`); the other styles are `p`. The text is centred vertically and clamped (`line-clamp`, with an ellipsis) to the whole lines that fit; when it is cut, its full text is the `title`. A card with its border off has no background, border or shadow, and no horizontal padding, so its text lines up with the other cards' edges; in edit mode it shows a faint dashed outline and tint so it can be found, dragged and resized. A borderless card is not a labelled group (the text speaks for itself).
- **Default layout:** first row `greeting` (a Text card: "Welcome back, {user}", `h1`, left, no border) at 0,0, 12x1 (10x1 since the Clock card took the top-right two cells, see "Clock card"); every other default card one row lower. The rest of the default (owner's dashboard, 2026-10-28): rows 1 to 4 hold Properties and Vehicles (3x4 each), People over Equipment (4x2 each) and, in the right-hand column, Service Activity (2x1, the flat calendar), Records (2x1) and the Spending Breakdown (2x2, last 12 months); rows 5 to 8 hold Intake and Reminders (3x4), Planned Spend over Expiring Warranties (3x2) and Spending Trend (3x4). Active Warranties and Due Maintenance are not in the default (they remain add-on cards). The phone layout is derived from this. The customized dashboard has no page header: the Edit button (admins only) is not beside a greeting any more (first a slim right-aligned row above the grid; now in the app's top bar, see `components/header-slot.tsx`); others get nothing above the grid. Saved layouts are not migrated (the feature was unreleased); Reset to default brings the greeting. The legacy dashboard keeps its own greeting. Several Text cards can be H1s; the default has exactly one.
- **Tokens:** one table, `TEXT_TOKENS` in `lib/dashboard/text-template.ts`, drives the settings dialog's help, the README's table (a test keeps them equal) and the loader. Grammar: `{name}` with a name of letters, digits and `_` (not starting with a digit), case-sensitive, not touching another brace. Known tokens are replaced; unknown ones (`{usr}`) render as typed and the dialog warns ("Not a known value") without blocking Save; double braces, braces with spaces and stray braces are plain text. A known token with no value renders empty and takes the ", " (else one " ") right before it, so "Welcome back, {user}" reads "Welcome back" with no name. Values are never read again as tokens.
- **Links (owner's request, 2026-10-27; as built):** a value about a page or an item is a link; the rest stay plain. `TEXT_TOKENS` gives each linked token `linksTo` (the destination's name, for the dialog and the README's "Links to" column), `href` (a static page: the one the neighbouring card's "view all" or the sidebar opens; a test checks each is a page under `app/(app)`) and, for a bare number or amount, `noun`.
  - Destinations: `{overdue}`, `{due_soon}` -> `/maintenance` (the Due Maintenance and Reminders cards' list); `{next_due}`, `{next_due_date}` -> the most urgent reminder's own edit dialog: its item's page on the Maintenance tab (Reminders for a person) with `?open=<reminder id>` (`dueTokenLinks` and `maintenanceEditHref`, from the loader); `{expiring_warranties}` -> `/warranties` (a count cannot open one item); `{next_expiring}`, `{next_expiring_date}` -> the soonest-ending warranty's edit dialog: its item's page on the Warranties tab with `?open=<warranty id>` (`expiringTokenLinks`, `warrantyEditHref`); `{intake}` -> `/intake`; `{spend_ytd}`, `{spend_month}` -> `/costs`; `{properties}`, `{vehicles}`, `{equipment}`, `{people}` -> `/assets/properties`, `/assets/vehicles`, `/assets/equipment`, `/assets/people` (People with Health on only: with it off the value is empty, so no link); `{records}` -> `/records`. Not links: `{user}`, `{user_full}`, `{date}`, `{weekday}`, `{year}`. List pages apply the device's property filter themselves.
  - Model: `renderSegments(text, values, links)` returns `Array<{ text } | { text, href, title, label? }>` (plain runs merged); `renderText` is its text joined, so the two cannot drift. A link's text is exactly its value; an empty value makes no link and takes the separator before it only from plain text (so a value ending in ", " right before an empty token now keeps that comma). Unknown tokens stay plain. Hrefs come only from the table or `dueTokenLinks`; both `renderSegments` and the view's reader (`textSegmentsOf`) keep a link only for a site path (`/x`, not `//x` or a scheme).
  - Look: the text's own size, weight and colour, a faint underline (`decoration-current/30`) that turns solid on hover and keyboard focus, a 2px `outline-ring` focus ring drawn inside the link (the clamped box clips outside it), and `pointer-coarse:py-1` so the tap target is about 24px without moving the text. Links sit inside the `h1`..`h4`/`p`, so heading semantics, wrapping, alignment, clamping and the cut-text `title` are unchanged.
  - Names: each link's `title` is "Open <destination>" ("Open Maintenance", "Open Furnace"); a link whose value is a bare number or amount gets `aria-label` "<value> <noun>" ("14 pieces of equipment", "$385.00 spent this month"), which starts with the visible text; a name or date is its own accessible name.
  - Edit mode: the card frame's `inert` content makes the links unclickable and unfocusable, like every card's links.
  - You & time: `{user}` (first word of the signed-in user's name), `{user_full}`, `{date}` (today in the regional date format), `{weekday}` (English), `{year}`.
  - Attention, from the Due Maintenance card's list (`dueMaintenance`, its 30-day window): `{overdue}`, `{due_soon}` (due but not overdue, so the two add up to the Due card's count), `{next_due}` and `{next_due_date}` (its first item; the date is empty for an item due by mileage); `{expiring_warranties}` (ending today through 60 days on, as Expiring Warranties counts by default); `{next_expiring}` and `{next_expiring_date}` (that card's first row: soonest end date, ties by id; the date from the stored UTC date as the card shows it; both empty when none ends in the window); `{intake}` (the Intake card's count).
  - Money: `{spend_ytd}` (the Spending Trend headline's year-to-date total), `{spend_month}` (the first of this month through today), in the household currency.
  - Counts: `{properties}`, `{vehicles}`, `{equipment}`, `{records}`, `{people}` (Health only; empty with Health off, and the dialog says "needs Health on").
- **Loading:** the `text` loader parses the text, collects the data needs of its known tokens and runs one count query per need, in parallel, and none for plain text, `{user}` or the date. Every count follows the device's property filter and Health gating like the neighbouring card. A need that fails leaves its tokens empty (logged); the card still renders. The data sent to the browser is the finished text as segments plus the same text as a string (`textCardData`: `{ text, segments }`). The four item links come from the cached due list and a cached first-row warranty lookup (`findFirst`, the card's filter and order), run only when the text uses one of those tokens; a failed link load leaves those values plain text.
  - **Deep links:** an asset page already opens a list item's edit dialog from `?tab=<tab>&open=<id>`; the item tokens use it (`lib/deep-link.ts`). The Maintenance and Warranties list pages have no edit dialog of their own (their rows link to the same asset-page URLs), so the links go there directly. `open` only selects a row the page already loaded and scoped, so an unknown or deleted id, an item hidden by the property filter (the page shows its usual hidden-by-filter notice) or a person-owned item with Health off (the person page is hidden) opens nothing and shows no error; the app has no roles, so every signed-in user can edit. After opening, the maintenance and warranty lists remove `open` from the address with `history.replaceState` (no navigation), so reload or Back does not reopen it and closing the dialog does not reopen it. `LoaderContext` carries `userName` (from the session, in the page and in `loadDashboardCards`).
- **Settings dialog:** a textarea with a live character count, Style (select), Align (segmented), Border (checkbox); a preview with sample values (linked values underlined, as spans, not links); "Live values", grouped, each a button that inserts its token at the caret (keyboard too), with availability notes and, for a linked token, a link icon and "Links to <destination>".

## Clock card (owner's request, 2026-10-27; as built)

- **Card:** type `clock`, label "Clock", icon `CalendarClock`, ungated, picker group "Text & time" (the Text group, renamed, Text first). Not data-backed: its loader returns null and runs no query. Min 1x1, max 4x4, added at 2x1 (2x1 full width on phones). Phone presets: Small 1x1 (half width), Medium 2x1, Large 2x3.
- **Time and formats:** the viewer's device time (`new Date()` in the browser; no time-zone setting). 12 or 24 hour and the date format come from Settings > Regional through the page's formatter (`useFormat()`; `timeParts` and `hour12` were added to `createFormatter`); the card has no format option of its own, and its settings say so. Weekday names are English, like the Text card's `{weekday}`.
- **Config** (`clockConfigSchema` in `lib/dashboard/clock.ts`; defaults filled, unknown keys dropped, validated on save like every card): `showSeconds` (default off), `showDate` (default on), `face`: `auto` (default; the analog face from 2 rows), `always` (also beside the time on a one-row card 3+ columns wide), `never` (digital only).
- **Looks** (`clockLook` in `lib/dashboard/clock-layout.ts`, from grid cells; a phone column counts as two): 1 column: the time (AM/PM under it when that makes the time clearly bigger; a short date once the card is tall enough); 2+ columns, 1 row: the time over the date; 2+ columns, 2+ rows: an analog face with the time and date beside it (wide cards) or under it (others), whichever gives the bigger face. `fitClock` sizes the look to the measured content box: the biggest time that fits, the longest date form that fits ("Tuesday, Oct 27, 2026", "Tue, Oct 27, 2026", "Oct 27, 2026", "Oct 27"), the face never under the Edit button, and a face below 56px dropped for the digital look. The view places each part at the boxes `layoutBoxes` computes, so the tests check what renders. Catalog tiers (`time` 1x1, `standard` 2x1, `face` 2x2) drive the picker's "Time -> Date -> Face" and the tier fade; a look change fades in too.
- **Design:** the time is the hero (heading font, semibold, tabular numerals); seconds and AM/PM small and muted after it (stacked, AM/PM on the baseline, when both); the date a calm muted line. The face: a hairline dial, 12 ticks with the quarters heavier, fine minute marks from 150px, hour and minute hands in `--foreground`, the second hand (only with seconds on) in `--primary`. Colours are theme tokens only. Hands step, never sweep; nothing animates under reduced motion.
- **Ticking:** one shared timer per granularity (`components/dashboard/cards/use-clock-time.ts`, `useSyncExternalStore`): each tick is aimed at the next wall-clock second (seconds on) or minute (`nextTickDelay`), so it never drifts; paused while the page is hidden, caught up at once when it shows. Only the clocks re-render. The face's `aria-label` ("Clock, 9:42 PM") changes once a minute. The server renders no time (it would be the server's clock): the card's box is fixed by the grid, so the time appearing after hydration shifts nothing.
- **Edit dashboard control:** a corner tab, not a button (owner's request, 2026-10-28). A triangle with a wrench sits in the top-right corner of the page's scrolling area, touching the header bar and clear of the scrollbar, and stays there as the page scrolls (`EditCorner` in `edit-context.tsx`, admins only; the server actions still check). Hovered or focused it slides out into a band reading "Edit dashboard" (a `clip-path` change between two four-point polygons; no motion under reduced motion). On touch the triangle is the whole control. It is hidden while editing (Done is in the edit bar), and after Done it takes focus back (`claimFocusReturn`). The clock card has no Edit button and the page has no other one; there is no fallback and no `?edit=1` shortcut.

## Behavior (decided)

- **A period of X months.** Where a card has a period, the settings dialog takes a typed month count. Spending Trend and Spending Breakdown: Period is This year, Last 12 months, or Last X months (a Months input, 1 to 60, shown only for the last choice); the headline, comparison (the same months a year earlier) and chart all use those months. The calendar cards (Service Activity, Health Visits & Expenses, Health Observations): Period is a month count, 1 to 36 (older configs' 6 or 12 still read). Planned Spend: Look ahead is a month count, 1 to 24 (older 6 or 12 still read). Config field kind `number` gained `showWhen` (shown only while a choice has a value).


- Not a feature flag any more: the customized dashboard is the only dashboard (the fixed one and the `customizedDashboardsEnabled` setting were removed; migration `drop_customized_dashboards_flag`).
- Only admins can enter edit mode and save layouts (`requireAdmin()`).
- The layout is stored once for the whole site (singleton row, like other settings models).

## Behavior (designer's calls — owner has not confirmed; mark and revisit)

- When first enabled, the dashboard looks like today's, rebuilt from the new cards, so nothing is blank. "Reset to default" returns to it at any time.
- Changes save automatically while editing; a clear "Done" leaves edit mode. No draft step.
- Fixed-column grid with snapping. Each card type declares min/max sizes and size tiers so content is never unreadable.
- Responsive: two saved layouts (phone, and tablet/desktop; see "Phone layout" below). Phones use a single column; editing on phone = long-press drag to reorder plus explicit move up/down buttons as a fallback, with height via drag handle or small/medium/large and width limited to what fits. Tablet and desktop get the full multi-column grid with free drag and edge-resize.
- Touch targets are large; controls (gear, remove, resize) are reachable by thumb.
- Content changes between size tiers should ease rather than jump.
- Safety nets: invalid layouts cannot be saved; deleted data or a disabled feature degrades a card into a clear message instead of an error; one failing card does not take down the page; "Reset to default" is always available.
- Cards fetch only their own data, so cards not on the dashboard cost nothing. (Today everything is fetched in one `Promise.all` in `app/(app)/page.tsx`.)
- Adding a card uses a "+ Add card" picker showing the available card types.

## Additional designer's calls (from builder check; owner has not confirmed)

- **Default layout = parity of content, not pixels.** Every current dashboard element must map to a card in the catalog (add a card type if one is missing). Any fixed header/greeting stays fixed and non-editable above the grid.
- **Disabled features:** cards for a disabled feature (People without health, Rooms without rooms) are hidden from the "+ Add card" picker and hidden from the rendered dashboard for everyone, including admins outside edit mode. If an already-placed card's feature is turned off, the card is skipped (not shown), and its saved config is kept. Skipping it closes only the rows it took; empty rows the admin left stay (see "Empty space" below). The stored layout is never changed by hiding.
- **Toggle off/on:** the saved layout is kept dormant when Customized Dashboards is turned off and reused when it is turned on again. Only "Reset to default" discards it.
- **Concurrent editing:** last-write-wins is not enough to be safe; saves carry a layout version, and a stale save is rejected with a friendly "dashboard changed elsewhere, reload" message. Autosave is debounced. The server rejects invalid layouts and also repairs layouts on load (unknown card types dropped, sizes clamped, overlaps resolved), so a bad stored layout can never break the page.
- **Phone layout (owner decided: separate):** phones have their own layout, stored alongside the tablet/desktop layout in the same site-wide record. Editing on a phone changes only the phone layout; editing on tablet/desktop changes only the large-screen layout. The set of cards and each card's config are shared; only order and size are per layout. When a card is added or removed, it is added to or removed from both layouts (a new card is appended at the end of the phone layout). If the phone layout has never been edited, it is derived from the large layout in row-major order (top to bottom, then left to right) until first edited. "Reset to default" resets both.
- **Empty space (owner decided, replaces gravity):** there is no gravity. Cards keep the row they are put on, so an admin can leave empty rows between cards, in edit mode and on the finished dashboard, on desktop and phone alike. Rules (pure engine in `lib/dashboard/grid.ts`):
  - *Gap cap:* at most 5 empty rows (`MAX_GAP_ROWS`) between a card and the lowest card above it in its columns, or the top of the page. `clampGaps` enforces it top to bottom (y, then x, then id): a card past the cap is pulled up to exactly the cap, never further, and nothing else moves. Save validation rejects a larger gap; repair on load applies the cap.
  - *Collisions push down:* a card that is dropped, resized or added wins its spot; only the cards it lands on are pushed down (cascading), never up. The rows it left stay empty.
  - *Live preview, not cumulative:* while dragging, the preview is the layout from when the drag began plus the dragged card at its current cell. Cards that slid out of the way slide back when the dragged card moves on; on drop, the preview is what is saved.
  - *Remove* leaves the space empty (the cap still applies below it). *Add* appends below the lowest card.
  - *Phone:* the same rules on the 2-column phone layout. A derived phone layout (never edited) is compact, in reading order. Phone "Move up / Move down" swaps a card with its neighbour by moving it into the neighbour's slot (the neighbour is pushed down), so gaps elsewhere are kept.
  - *Keyboard / Move & resize menu:* one cell per step under the same rules (a step down leaves an empty row); at the gap cap a step down jumps past the card below.
  - *Hidden cards (feature off, no view):* display only, `collapseHidden` closes exactly the rows a hidden card occupied in the columns of the cards below it; a card beside it keeps its gap. Existing compact layouts load unchanged.
- **Config:** per-card config stores IDs (e.g. property). If a configured item is deleted, the card shows a clear message with a one-tap way to reconfigure (admins) rather than silently clearing.
- **Cards respect existing access rules** of the pages they link to; the layout itself is shared.
- **Tests:** vitest for layout validation/repair/migration and size-tier selection; UI interaction checks as practical; verify all 12 themes visually.
